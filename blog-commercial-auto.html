Does personal auto insurance cover business use?

Short answer: usually not. If you use your car, pickup or van to make money, a personal policy may deny the claim — and you could pay the damage out of pocket.

By the 4 Savings Solutions team·[PUBLISH DATE]·Ler em português

Personal auto insurance is priced for commuting, errands and trips. Once a vehicle is used for business, the risk changes: more hours on the road, tools and cargo, employees driving, and more people who could sue. That's why most personal policies exclude or limit business use.

What counts as business use?

  • Driving to job sites with tools or materials
  • Delivering products or food for your own business
  • Transporting clients or employees
  • A vehicle registered or titled to your LLC or company

Commuting to a single office usually doesn't count. Rideshare and app delivery have their own rules, so ask your agent about those.

What Commercial Auto covers

A commercial auto policy covers liability for injuries and damage you cause to others, and can add collision and comprehensive for your own vehicle. It also covers employees who drive for you, and can include Hired & Non-Owned Auto for rented or personal cars used on the job.

Who usually needs it

Contractors, landscapers, cleaning companies, delivery businesses and owner-operator truckers. Trucking companies that cross state lines may also need federal filings like a USDOT number.

The bottom line

If your vehicle helps you earn money, assume your personal policy isn't enough until an agent confirms it. The cost of the right policy is small compared with paying for an accident yourself.
